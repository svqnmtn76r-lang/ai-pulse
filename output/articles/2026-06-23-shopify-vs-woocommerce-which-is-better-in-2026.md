## Shopify vs WooCommerce: What's the difference?

**Quick answer:** Shopify is a fully hosted SaaS platform with built-in hosting and support, while WooCommerce is a self-hosted WordPress plugin that requires more technical setup but offers greater flexibility.

## Overview

The choice between Shopify and WooCommerce remains one of the most common decisions facing new e-commerce entrepreneurs in 2026. Both platforms power millions of online stores globally, yet they serve fundamentally different needs. Shopify operates as a closed, managed ecosystem where the company handles hosting, security, and infrastructure. WooCommerce, by contrast, is an open-source plugin that transforms WordPress into a storefront, placing technical responsibility on the store owner or their development team.

This comparison matters because the decision affects not just initial setup costs, but long-term scalability, customization options, and operational overhead. Understanding where each platform excels helps business owners align their choice with their technical expertise and growth trajectory.

## Key differences explained

**Shopify's managed approach** means you pay a recurring fee but offload infrastructure concerns entirely. Shopify handles server maintenance, security patches, and compliance requirements. This appeals to founders who want to focus on marketing and sales rather than technical operations. The trade-off is that customization stays within Shopify's guardrails—you cannot access the underlying code or deploy custom server logic.

**WooCommerce's flexibility** comes from its open-source nature. Developers can modify virtually any aspect of the platform, integrate custom payment processors, or build bespoke features. However, this freedom demands technical expertise. You or your team must manage hosting, backups, security updates, and performance optimization. For growing businesses with development resources, WooCommerce can be significantly cheaper at scale.

## Operational considerations

Shopify excels for solo entrepreneurs and small teams with limited technical bandwidth. Its built-in tools for inventory, shipping integrations, and abandoned cart recovery reduce the need for third-party services. Payment processing is streamlined, with Shopify Payments available in many regions.

WooCommerce suits businesses that already use WordPress, have developer capacity, or require deep customization. Its open ecosystem means you can integrate nearly any service, but each integration adds complexity and potential points of failure.

## What happens next

Your decision should hinge on three factors: technical comfort level, budget constraints, and customization needs. If you prioritize speed-to-market and prefer monthly predictability, Shopify remains the stronger choice. If you value long-term cost control and deep customization, investigate WooCommerce further—but ensure you have hosting and development support in place.

For detailed pricing and feature breakdowns, visit Shopify's and WooCommerce's official documentation to compare current offerings.
