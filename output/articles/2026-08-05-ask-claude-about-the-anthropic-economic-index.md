## Key differences in practice

The connector shifts emphasis from *how* to access data toward *what questions* you can ask. A researcher investigating inflation trends can ask Claude directly rather than manually filtering datasets. Claude handles the interpretation layer, potentially offering context or comparative analysis alongside raw numbers.

However, traditional analytics platforms maintain advantages in scenarios demanding complex multi-variable analysis, custom visualization requirements, or institutional compliance needs. They also typically offer more granular filtering options and historical data spanning decades.

The connector particularly benefits small teams and individual researchers who lack dedicated data infrastructure. For enterprise environments already invested in BI platforms like Tableau or Power BI, the connector functions as an additional analysis layer rather than a replacement.
