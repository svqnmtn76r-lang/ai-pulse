## Shopify vs WooCommerce: What's the difference?

Shopify is a hosted SaaS platform with built-in infrastructure and support, while WooCommerce is a self-hosted WordPress plugin that requires your own server management—making them fundamentally different approaches to dropshipping.

## Overview

Choosing the right ecommerce platform is critical for dropshipping success in 2026. As more entrepreneurs launch online stores, the debate between fully managed solutions and self-hosted alternatives continues to shape the landscape. Shopify remains the market leader for dropshipping, but WooCommerce appeals to those seeking greater control and flexibility. Understanding their core differences helps you align platform choice with your business model, technical skills, and growth plans.

## Key differences explained

**Hosting and infrastructure**

Shopify handles everything behind the scenes—your store runs on Shopify's servers, meaning you don't manage servers, security patches, or uptime. WooCommerce requires you to rent hosting separately and maintain your WordPress installation yourself. For dropshipping beginners, Shopify's all-in-one approach eliminates infrastructure headaches.

**Dropshipping integrations**

Shopify's app ecosystem includes purpose-built dropshipping tools that sync inventory, automate orders, and manage supplier relationships. While WooCommerce supports similar plugins, Shopify's integrations tend to be tighter and more merchant-focused. If your dropshipping workflow depends on seamless automation, Shopify's native ecosystem provides stronger native support.

**Customization and control**

WooCommerce grants deeper customization for those comfortable with code. You can modify databases, write custom plugins, and own your entire tech stack. Shopify offers themes and apps but keeps core functionality proprietary. In our view, this trade-off means Shopify excels for faster launches while WooCommerce suits long-term builders willing to invest development time.

**Cost structure**

Shopify charges monthly subscription fees plus transaction fees on lower-tier plans, plus payment processing costs. WooCommerce itself is free, but you'll pay for hosting, SSL certificates, and premium plugins. Neither approach is inherently cheaper—total cost depends on your traffic, order volume, and feature choices. We recommend calculating realistic costs for your projected sales volume before deciding.

**Technical demands**

Shopify requires minimal technical knowledge. WooCommerce demands familiarity with WordPress, PHP, and server administration—or budget for a developer. For solo founders without technical backgrounds, Shopify reduces friction significantly.

## What happens next

Your choice depends on priority: rapid deployment and managed infrastructure favor Shopify, while cost control and deep customization favor WooCommerce. Most successful dropshippers starting today choose Shopify for its dropshipping-optimized ecosystem, though experienced developers often prefer WooCommerce's flexibility.

Compare pricing and features directly on both platforms' official sites, then consider running a test store on your preferred option before committing resources.
