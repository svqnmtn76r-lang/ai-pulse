## What happened

The US government has issued an export control directive targeting two advanced AI models developed by Anthropic: Fable 5 and Mythos 5. The directive mandates a complete suspension of access for all foreign nationals, regardless of their physical location—whether abroad or within US territory [anthropic.com].

This marks a significant shift in AI export policy, placing restrictions directly on model access rather than just hardware or training infrastructure. The move suggests growing concern over the proliferation of advanced AI capabilities across international borders, aligning with broader US efforts to maintain technological advantage in artificial intelligence.

Anthropic announced the compliance measure in a statement addressing the policy shift. The company will implement technical controls to enforce the access restrictions, effectively creating a geofenced or identity-verified system for Fable 5 and Mythos 5.

The directive's scope is notably broad—it doesn't exempt researchers, academics, or non-commercial users, indicating the government views these models as sensitive technology requiring strict control. This approach differs from previous export regulations that often carved out exceptions for educational or research purposes.

Organizations relying on these models will need to reassess their international workflows and partnerships. Teams with distributed workforces across borders may face operational constraints. The precedent could trigger similar restrictions on other advanced AI systems, particularly as the US continues refining its AI export control framework.

For the broader AI industry, this directive signals that model-level restrictions are now part of the policy toolkit, potentially influencing how companies structure access to cutting-edge systems and plan international expansion strategies.
