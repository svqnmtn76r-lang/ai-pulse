# Shopify vs BigCommerce: What's the difference?

**Quick answer:** Shopify prioritizes ease of use and speed to launch, while BigCommerce emphasizes advanced customization and enterprise-scale features for larger merchants.

## Overview

As ecommerce platforms mature in 2026, merchants face an increasingly crowded marketplace of options. Shopify remains the category leader, but serious competitors have emerged for businesses with specific needs. Understanding how Shopify compares to alternatives like BigCommerce matters because the wrong platform choice can limit growth, inflate operational costs, or create technical friction down the line.

This comparison examines two of the most established hosted ecommerce solutions. Both Shopify and BigCommerce serve merchants across small to enterprise segments, but they appeal to different buyer personas and business models. The choice between them often hinges on technical depth, budget constraints, and long-term scalability requirements.

## Key differences explained

**Ease of use:** Shopify's interface is deliberately simplified for non-technical merchants. Dashboard navigation, theme customization, and app installation follow intuitive patterns. BigCommerce, while user-friendly, exposes more advanced settings by default, requiring familiarity with concepts like webhooks, API endpoints, and server-side logic.

**Pricing model:** Shopify uses straightforward tiered monthly plans with transaction fees on basic tiers. BigCommerce operates on tiered monthly subscriptions without per-transaction fees, which can become advantageous for high-volume sellers. Neither model is universally superior—it depends on your sales volume and transaction patterns.

**Customization capabilities:** Shopify offers customization through its app marketplace and Liquid templating language. For most merchants, this suffices. However, developers building unique workflows often find BigCommerce's API-first architecture more flexible. BigCommerce's webhooks and custom endpoints allow deeper integration with external systems.

**Scalability trajectory:** Shopify handles growth from zero to millions in annual revenue smoothly, with infrastructure scaling automatically. BigCommerce historically positioned itself as the enterprise alternative, though Shopify's Plus tier now competes directly in that segment. Both platforms scale reliably at high transaction volumes.

**Multi-channel integration:** Both platforms excel at selling across marketplaces. Shopify's native connections to Amazon, TikTok, and Google Shopping are well-established. BigCommerce offers comparable integrations with similar sophistication and feature parity.

## What happens next

The ecommerce platform landscape continues to fragment. Shopify's dominance remains secure for merchants prioritizing rapid deployment and simplicity. BigCommerce and similar platforms capture share among businesses that demand lower transaction fees, deeper customization, or enterprise-grade support from day one.

When evaluating platforms in 2026, focus on your team's technical capacity, expected transaction volume, and customization requirements rather than brand recognition alone. A free trial period—offered by both Shopify and BigCommerce—remains the best way to assess fit before committing.
