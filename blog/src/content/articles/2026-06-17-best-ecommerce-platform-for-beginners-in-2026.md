## Shopify vs WooCommerce: What's the difference?

Shopify is a fully hosted SaaS platform, while WooCommerce is a self-hosted WordPress plugin—meaning Shopify handles infrastructure for you, but WooCommerce requires more hands-on management.

## Overview

For beginners entering ecommerce in 2026, choosing the right platform is crucial. The decision often comes down to two popular options: Shopify, a comprehensive hosted solution, and WooCommerce, a plugin-based approach built on WordPress. Both serve different needs depending on your technical comfort level, budget flexibility, and growth ambitions.

Shopify has established itself as a leader in the beginner-friendly space by handling hosting, security, and infrastructure automatically. This all-in-one approach means you can launch a store quickly without worrying about server management or technical backend issues. WooCommerce, conversely, appeals to those who already use WordPress or prefer greater customization control, though it demands more technical involvement.

Understanding the distinction matters because it shapes your entire operational experience. With Shopify, you're paying for convenience and support. With WooCommerce, you're investing time and potentially hiring developers to achieve similar results.

## Key differences explained

**Hosting and infrastructure**: Shopify eliminates the complexity of server management. The platform handles uptime, backups, security patches, and scalability automatically. WooCommerce requires you to source hosting separately—either through budget options or premium providers—and you're responsible for maintaining it.

**Cost transparency**: Shopify operates on predictable subscription tiers. WooCommerce costs vary based on hosting selection, premium plugins, and whether you hire developers. For absolute beginners, Shopify's fixed pricing often feels more manageable, though WooCommerce can be cheaper at smaller scales.

**Customization depth**: WooCommerce offers deeper customization since you control the underlying code. Shopify provides strong customization through its theme and app marketplace, but true code-level changes require Shopify's Liquid templating language or hiring a developer.

**Learning curve**: Shopify prioritizes simplicity. Its dashboard, inventory management, and order fulfillment are designed for non-technical users. WooCommerce requires WordPress familiarity and comfort with plugins—a steeper initial climb for true beginners.

## What happens next

If you're starting fresh and value simplicity, Shopify's hosted model removes friction from launch day. If you're already invested in WordPress or need granular control, WooCommerce remains flexible. Visit Shopify's official pricing page and WooCommerce's documentation to evaluate specific features matching your product category, expected traffic, and integration needs. Both platforms will scale with your business—the choice hinges on whether you want managed simplicity or hands-on control.
