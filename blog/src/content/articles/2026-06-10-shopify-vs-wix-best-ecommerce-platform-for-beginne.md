## Shopify vs Wix: What's the difference?

**Quick answer:** Shopify is a dedicated ecommerce platform built for scalability and sales, while Wix is a website builder with ecommerce features bolted on—making Shopify generally better for merchants prioritizing online sales.

## Overview

Choosing an ecommerce platform as a beginner can feel overwhelming. The two names that come up most often are Shopify and Wix, but they solve different problems. Understanding their core design philosophies helps you pick the right tool for your business.

Shopify started as a pure-play ecommerce solution and has evolved into a comprehensive commerce ecosystem. Wix, by contrast, began as a website builder and added ecommerce capabilities later. This distinction matters because it shapes how each platform approaches store management, payment processing, inventory, and scaling.

For beginners in 2026, the choice between them often comes down to whether you're primarily building a store or primarily building a website with some products attached.

## Key differences explained

**Shopify's commerce-first approach** means its core features—abandoned cart recovery, order management, shipping integrations, and multi-channel selling—are purpose-built for merchants. Shopify lets you sync inventory across your store, social channels, and marketplaces from one dashboard.

**Wix's website-builder DNA** shines when you need design flexibility without coding. Its drag-and-drop editor is genuinely intuitive, and beginners can create a visually compelling site quickly. However, ecommerce on Wix feels like a secondary feature. Inventory management is simpler, and payment gateway options are more limited.

For beginners asking "which is easier?"—that depends on your priority. If you're comfortable with templates and want a faster visual setup, Wix's drag-and-drop approach may feel smoother. If you're ready to think operationally about order management, shipping, and growth, Shopify's interface rewards that mindset.

**Payment processing** favors Shopify. While both accept credit cards, Shopify Payments is tightly integrated and reduces friction. Shopify also connects to 100+ payment providers, giving you options as you expand internationally or add alternative payment methods. Wix's payment ecosystem is narrower.

**Customization and extensions** matter long-term. Shopify's app store has thousands of plugins for accounting, marketing, fulfillment, and analytics. Wix's ecosystem is growing but smaller. In our view, this gap widens if your business needs evolve beyond basics.

## What happens next

Start by asking yourself: Am I building a store, or am I building a website that sells? If you're serious about ecommerce as your core business model, Shopify's merchant-focused toolset and scaling potential make it the stronger choice. If you're a creative professional adding a shop to an existing web presence, Wix may feel more natural.

Both platforms offer free trials. Hands-on testing with a few products is the fastest way to see which mental model fits your workflow.
