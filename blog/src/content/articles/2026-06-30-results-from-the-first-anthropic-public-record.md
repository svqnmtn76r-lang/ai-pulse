# Anthropic Releases First Public Record on AI Attitudes: What American Opinion Surveys Reveal

Anthropic, the AI safety company behind Claude, has launched the Anthropic Public Record—a comprehensive national survey measuring how Americans perceive and feel about artificial intelligence. Released as an ongoing research initiative, this effort represents a systematic attempt to capture public sentiment on AI at a critical moment when the technology is rapidly advancing and becoming more integrated into everyday life.

The significance of this undertaking lies in creating a baseline understanding of public opinion that can inform both industry practices and policy discussions. As AI systems become more prevalent, understanding what citizens think about their deployment, safety, and regulation becomes increasingly important for responsible development.

## TL;DR

- **Public sentiment baseline**: The survey captures Americans' attitudes toward AI adoption, safety concerns, and trust in different institutions managing AI development
- **Ongoing measurement**: Unlike one-time polls, the Public Record is designed as a recurring survey to track how opinions shift over time as technology evolves
- **Impact**: Results provide data that can inform how companies develop AI responsibly and help policymakers understand constituent concerns about regulation and safety

## Background

Public opinion research on emerging technologies typically lags behind their development. The internet, social media, and mobile computing each created significant societal changes before comprehensive public attitude surveys were conducted. By the time surveys existed, many design decisions were already locked in.

AI presents a different opportunity. As the technology is still in relatively early stages of mainstream deployment, there's a window to measure public sentiment before widespread adoption becomes inevitable. Previous polling on AI has been sporadic and often conducted by organizations with specific agendas, making it difficult to establish truly independent baselines.

Anthropic's decision to create a public record addresses this gap. Rather than relying on disparate surveys from different organizations using different methodologies, the Public Record aims to provide consistent, comparable data over time. This matters for researchers, policymakers, and companies trying to understand whether they're developing AI in ways that align with public values and concerns.

## How it Works

### Survey Methodology and Scope

The Anthropic Public Record functions as a national survey designed to capture representative samples of American public opinion. The survey methodology employs standard social science research practices to ensure results are generalizable to the broader population rather than reflecting only the views of particular demographics.

The survey covers a range of topics related to AI, including public awareness of AI systems, perceived benefits and risks, trust in different institutions (technology companies, government, academia) to responsibly develop and deploy AI, and attitudes toward potential regulation. By casting a wide net across multiple dimensions of AI perception, the survey captures not just whether people support or oppose AI, but the nuanced reasoning behind their positions.

Conducting this research as a public record—meaning the methodology and results are made available to the public—distinguishes this approach from proprietary polling that companies might conduct for internal use only. This transparency enables other researchers to evaluate the findings, conduct meta-analyses, and build on the work.

### Measuring Attitudes Over Time

A key innovation of the Public Record is its design as a recurring survey rather than a one-time measurement. Public opinion doesn't remain static; it shifts as technologies mature, as incidents occur, and as public understanding deepens. By conducting the survey regularly using consistent questions and methodology, researchers can track these shifts and identify inflection points where sentiment changes.

This longitudinal approach reveals whether growing familiarity with AI breeds comfort (the "mere exposure effect") or concern (as people encounter problems), and how major events—regulatory announcements, AI breakthroughs, or incidents—influence public perception. These trends have implications for how companies should approach transparency and for policymakers considering regulation timing.

### Data Applications and Access

The results from the survey are made publicly available, enabling researchers, policymakers, technologists, and journalists to understand public sentiment. This accessibility serves multiple purposes: it provides companies developing AI systems with evidence about what concerns matter most to the public, it gives advocates and policymakers quantitative backing for their positions, and it creates a shared factual basis for discussions about AI policy.

By publishing findings openly, Anthropic positions itself as contributing to the broader societal conversation about AI rather than simply commissioning research to advance its own interests. This approach—similar to how some technology companies publish annual AI safety or responsible AI research—helps establish credibility in discussions about how the industry should be governed.

## What Happens Next

The inaugural Anthropic Public Record provides a baseline for future surveys. Subsequent iterations will reveal whether public confidence in AI is increasing or declining, whether particular concerns (job displacement, bias, misuse) are becoming more or less salient, and whether trust in specific institutions (tech companies, government, academic institutions) is shifting.

These insights matter because they reflect the values and concerns of the people who will ultimately live with AI systems in their workplaces, homes, and communities. As AI becomes more powerful and consequential, ensuring that its development reflects public values—rather than surprising the public with capabilities or deployment—becomes increasingly important.

The Public Record also establishes a research precedent. Other organizations may conduct similar surveys, creating a richer landscape of public opinion data. Over time, this allows for comparison across different survey efforts and helps distinguish genuine public sentiment from methodology artifacts or framing effects.

For anyone tracking AI's societal integration—whether as a technologist, policymaker, investor, or informed citizen—the Anthropic Public Record provides a resource for understanding the human dimension of AI advancement. As the technology continues to develop rapidly, staying attuned to public sentiment becomes as important as tracking technical progress.
