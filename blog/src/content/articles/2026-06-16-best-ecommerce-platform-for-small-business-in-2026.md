## Shopify vs WooCommerce: What's the difference?

Shopify is a fully hosted SaaS platform, while WooCommerce is a self-hosted WordPress plugin—making Shopify faster to launch but WooCommerce more customizable for technical users.

## Overview

For small business owners evaluating ecommerce platforms in 2026, the choice between Shopify and WooCommerce remains one of the most consequential decisions in setting up online sales. Both platforms power millions of stores worldwide, but they serve different business needs and technical comfort levels.

Shopify handles hosting, security, and infrastructure maintenance automatically, letting founders focus on product and marketing. WooCommerce, by contrast, requires you to manage your own WordPress hosting and updates—but grants deeper control over your storefront's code and integrations. Understanding these trade-offs matters because the wrong choice can mean painful migration later or leaving money on the table through unnecessary complexity.

## Key differences explained

**Hosting and infrastructure**: Shopify eliminates infrastructure headaches by managing servers, SSL certificates, and security patches. This convenience comes at a monthly cost. WooCommerce leaves you responsible for finding a hosting provider, managing backups, and handling security updates yourself—though this also means you avoid platform lock-in.

**Customization depth**: Shopify's Liquid templating language and theme system work well for most merchants, but they impose boundaries. WooCommerce grants direct access to PHP and database layers, making it ideal if you need custom checkout flows, proprietary integrations, or unusual business logic.

**Total cost of ownership**: Shopify's subscription pricing is straightforward but compounds over time. WooCommerce's plugin ecosystem is free, but quality hosting, security plugins, and backup solutions add up. For very small stores, Shopify may cost less; for established businesses with custom needs, WooCommerce can prove more economical.

**Payment processing**: Shopify's native integration with Shopify Payments streamlines checkout and reduces transaction fees for many regions. WooCommerce requires separate payment gateway plugins, adding setup friction but offering more flexibility in choosing providers.

## Which is right for your business?

Choose **Shopify** if you want to launch quickly, prefer managed services, or lack technical resources to maintain WordPress. It's in our view the better choice for founders prioritizing speed-to-market and peace of mind.

Choose **WooCommerce** if you already use WordPress, need deep customization, have technical support available, or want to avoid platform dependency. It suits established businesses with specific feature requirements.
